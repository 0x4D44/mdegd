## 1. PERTH CITY STATUS

**Did Perth get city status?** Yes, but in 2012 -- not 2022.

- Perth was granted city status on 14 March 2012 to mark the Queen's Diamond Jubilee (not the Platinum Jubilee).
- It was the only Scottish town to enter the UK-wide city status competition, which launched in December 2010.
- The bid was submitted in May 2011. Perth was one of 26 bidders; it won alongside Chelmsford and St Asaph.
- Perth became Scotland's seventh city.

**Before city status:** Perth held the title "City and Royal Burgh" historically. Royal burgh status was granted by King William the Lion in the early 12th century. Perth was the capital of Scotland from the 9th century until 1437, then officially Scotland's second city until 1975 local government reorganisation stripped the title.

**2022 Platinum Jubilee:** Perth already had city status, so did not compete again. Dunfermline won city status in 2022 to mark the Platinum Jubilee, becoming Scotland's eighth city.

**Quiz angles:**
- Q: In what year was Perth granted city status -- 2012 or 2022? (A: 2012, Diamond Jubilee)
- Q: Which Scottish town won city status in the 2022 Platinum Jubilee competition? (A: Dunfermline)
- Q: What was Perth's status before 2012? (A: Royal Burgh / it lost city status in 1975 local government reorganisation)
- Q: How many cities does Scotland now have? (A: 8 -- Glasgow, Edinburgh, Dundee, Aberdeen, Stirling, Inverness, Perth, Dunfermline)

## 2. PERTH MUSEUM & THE STONE OF DESTINY

**The Museum:**
- Perth Museum opened 30 March 2024 in the former Perth City Hall.
- Cost: GBP 27 million (GBP 17m from Perth & Kinross Council, GBP 10m from Tay Cities Region Deal). Original budget was GBP 23m but rose by GBP 4m.
- Architect: Mecanoo (Dutch firm). Key design feature: a "Vennel" -- a symbolic passage cutting through the building connecting the Main Hall and Lesser Hall, with imposing bronze doors crafted on the Black Isle.
- The City Hall dates from 1914 (Edwardian era), originally designed by Harry Clifford and Thomas Lunan.
- Showcases 3,000 local objects spanning 10,000 years.
- Opening exhibition: "Unicorn" -- first major UK exhibition on Scotland's national animal.
- Contains Bonnie Prince Charlie's sword and a rare Jacobite wine glass (both on public display for the first time).

**Visitor success:**
- 100,000 visitors in the first 100 days (smashed projections).
- Over 250,000 visitors in the first year, exceeding the annual target of 167,000 by 50%.
- Perth & Kinross Council recorded 68% increase in footfall comparing 2024 vs 2023.
- Won Cultural Building of the Year at the Archello Awards, plus several other awards.

**The Stone of Destiny (Stone of Scone):**
- A 25-inch block of red sandstone used for Scottish coronations from 1249 onwards.
- Originally kept at Scone Abbey, near Perth. All Scottish kings were enthroned on it atop Moot Hill.
- Seized by Edward I in 1296 and taken to Westminster Abbey, fitted into the Coronation Chair.
- 1950: Four Glasgow University students (Ian Hamilton, Gavin Vernon, Kay Matheson, Alan Stuart) stole it from Westminster Abbey on Christmas Day 1950. It broke into two pieces. They left it at Arbroath Abbey in April 1951, draped in a Scottish flag. No one was prosecuted.
- 1996: Returned to Scotland by PM John Major, displayed at Edinburgh Castle.
- 2023: Temporarily sent to Westminster Abbey for the coronation of King Charles III (6 May 2023).
- March 2024: Moved from Edinburgh Castle to Perth Museum -- first time back in Perthshire in 700 years. Displayed in a two-storey oak timber box in the former auditorium. Free to view.
- July 2025: A man in a kilt tried to break the display case. No damage to the Stone, but the Stone of Destiny Experience was closed for a period.

**Quiz angles:**
- Q: What building was converted into Perth Museum? (A: Perth City Hall)
- Q: How much did the Perth Museum transformation cost? (A: GBP 27 million)
- Q: Which Dutch architecture firm designed Perth Museum? (A: Mecanoo)
- Q: How many visitors did Perth Museum receive in its first year? (A: Over 250,000)
- Q: When was the Stone of Destiny stolen from Westminster Abbey by students? (A: Christmas Day 1950)
- Q: Where was the Stone displayed before Perth Museum? (A: Edinburgh Castle, since 1996)
- Q: For what 2023 event was the Stone temporarily returned to Westminster? (A: Coronation of King Charles III)
- Q: How long had the Stone been away from Perthshire before returning in 2024? (A: 700 years)

## 3. PERTH HIGH STREET / ST JOHN'S CENTRE

**St John's Shopping Centre:**
- Opened in 1988. Located between South Street, King Edward Street, Scott Street, and the pedestrianised High Street.
- A radical plan proposed part-demolishing the centre to open up the area around the new Perth Museum.

## 4. PERTH FLOOD DEFENCES

**Perth city flood defences:** The GBP 25m Perth Flood Prevention Scheme was completed in 2001. Issues persist:
- September 2023: Flooding devastated parts of Perth. The council admitted they should have closed the South Inch floodgates earlier.
- Storm Babet (October 2023): Major flooding. Flood gates were closed on Wednesday 18 October. Around 170mm of rain fell at Waterside Perth in 24 hours. Dalguise Activity Centre near Perth forced to close until 2024.

**Comrie Flood Protection Scheme:**
- GBP 27 million scheme to protect 189 homes and businesses in Comrie.
- Funded 80% by the Scottish Government, 20% by Perth & Kinross Council.
- Statutory approval granted December 2021.
- Detailed design completed spring 2023.
- Contractor: Balfour Beatty (awarded contract August 2024).
- Construction started October 2024. Completion expected Autumn 2026.
- Will construct 2.8 km of river walls and embankments along the Ruchill River, River Earn, and River Lednock.

**Dunkeld Flood Protection Study:** Conclusions approved May 2023.

**Quiz angles:**
- Q: How much did the Comrie Flood Protection Scheme cost? (A: GBP 27 million)
- Q: Which three rivers does the Comrie scheme protect against? (A: Ruchill, Earn, Lednock)
- Q: What did the council admit they failed to do during the September 2023 Perth flooding? (A: Close the South Inch floodgates in time)

## 6. COUNCIL BUDGET & COUNCIL TAX

**Council tax rises:**
- 2023/24: 3.9% rise (passed 24-14, SNP + Lib Dems + Independents vs Conservatives, Labour abstained).
- 2025/26: 9.5% rise -- the biggest council tax rise in Perth and Kinross history. Driven by National Insurance increases and social care costs.
- 2026/27: 8.9% rise, taking Band D to GBP 1,673.84 per year.
- Council was planning on the assumption of 10% rises for future years.

**Second homes council tax:**
- December 2023: Council unanimously approved 100% council tax premium on second homes (effective 1 April 2024). Owners now pay 200% of normal council tax.
- 1,160 second homes in Perth & Kinross. Highland Perthshire has 488 (the most).
- Expected to generate over GBP 2m additional income.
- Purpose-built holiday homes and job-related dwellings retain a protected 50% discount.
- From 2025/26, half the money goes to affordable/social housing in rural areas.

- Q: How much extra do second home owners in Perth & Kinross now pay in council tax? (A: 100% premium / double / 200%)
- Q: How many second homes are there in Perth and Kinross? (A: About 1,160)
- Q: Which ward has the most second homes? (A: Highland Perthshire, with 488)

## 7. ROADS AND TRANSPORT

**A9 Dualling (Perth to Inverness):**
- Originally promised for completion by 2025. Now delayed 10 years -- full completion expected end of 2035.
- As of October 2025: 35% dualled. Will reach 48% by end of 2030, 85% by end of 2033, 100% by end of 2035.
- Tay Crossing to Ballinluig: under construction, expected operational by end of 2028.
- Tomatin to Moy: under construction, expected operational by Spring 2028.
- Pass of Birnam to Tay Crossing: the last section to start statutory process (draft orders published May 2025).
- January 2026: Government confirmed remaining sections will be delivered using capital-funded design and build contracts.

**Cross Tay Link Road (CTLR):**
- 6 km (3.7 miles) road linking A93/A94 near Scone to the A9 north of Inveralmond Roundabout.
- Centrepiece: Destiny Bridge -- a 307.4m, three-span bridge over the Highland Mainline Railway, the River Tay, and its floodplain. Includes a parallel cycle path.
- Also includes 2 km of realigned dual carriageway on the A9 north of Inveralmond.
- Cost: GBP 150 million (originally estimated GBP 118m, revised upward).
- Contractor: BAM Nuttall.
- Opened: 31 March 2025. Delivered on time and within (revised) budget.
- Purpose: reduce traffic congestion in Perth city centre, especially the A989 Inner Ring Road and Bridgend.

**A9/A85 Junction Improvement:** New link road from A85 Crieff Road across A9 and River Almond into Bertha Park, plus improved A9/A85 junction. Opened 4 months ahead of schedule.

**Quiz angles:**
- Q: What is the name of the new bridge over the River Tay on the Cross Tay Link Road? (A: Destiny Bridge)
- Q: How much did the Cross Tay Link Road cost? (A: GBP 150 million)
- Q: When did it open? (A: 31 March 2025)
- Q: The A9 dualling was originally promised for completion in 2025. When is it now expected? (A: 2035 -- a 10-year delay)
- Q: What percentage of the A9 between Perth and Inverness was dualled as of October 2025? (A: 35%)

## 9. PERTH THEATRE / CONCERT HALL

**Perth Theatre:** Underwent GBP 16.6m renovation 2016-17 by architect Richard Murphy. Scotland's oldest repertory theatre. Reopened September 2017.

**Horsecross Arts (operator):**
- Charitable organisation running both Perth Theatre and Perth Concert Hall since 2005.
- Serious financial crisis in 2019: GBP 330,000 cash flow deterioration, GBP 74,000 shortfall, mass board resignations including chairman Magnus Linklater. Council bailout required.
- Former chairman said the council "seriously underestimated" the cost of running the GBP 15m Perth Theatre.
- 120 jobs at risk during redundancy talks.
- New Director Christopher Glasgow appointed permanently November 2023.
- Net assets at March 2024: GBP 933k (down from GBP 1.16m in 2023).

## 11. RECYCLING / WASTE

**Twin Stream Recycling (November 2023):**
- Perth & Kinross switched from single mixed recycling to "twin stream":
  - Blue bins: paper & cardboard only
  - New grey-lidded bins: plastics, cans, tins
- Grey bin deliveries started August 2023; all distributed by January 2024.

**Energy from Waste:** From August 2023, all non-recyclable waste from green bins and recycling centre skips sent to an Energy from Waste facility (burned to create energy) instead of landfill.

**New build charging:** From 1 April 2024, newly built properties must pay for bin delivery.

**Quiz angles:**
- Q: What colour are the new recycling bins introduced in Perth & Kinross for plastics and cans? (A: Grey-lidded)
- Q: What goes in a blue bin since the 2023 change? (A: Paper and cardboard only)
- Q: Since August 2023, what happens to non-recyclable waste in Perth & Kinross? (A: Sent to an Energy from Waste facility / burned)

## 19. SPORT

**St Johnstone FC:**
- 2021: Historic cup double -- won Scottish League Cup (beat Livingston 1-0) and Scottish Cup (beat Hibernian 1-0). First team outside the Old Firm to win a cup double since Aberdeen in 1990. 10,000/1 odds. Shaun Rooney scored the winner in both finals.
- Subsequent seasons: struggled badly.
- May 2025: RELEGATED from the Scottish Premiership for the first time since 2002 (lost 2-1 to Hearts).

**Perth Racecourse:**
- Located in the grounds of Scone Palace. Scotland's most northerly racecourse.
- Specialises in National Hunt (jump) racing, April to October.
- 2023: Scone Palace and Perth Racecourse MERGED. Scone Estates invested a minimum of GBP 5m into the racecourse over 8 years. This was described as a "transformational deal" ensuring preservation of horse racing at Perth.

**Dewar's Centre (Ice Rink) / PH2O:**
- Dewar's Centre, Bell's Sports Centre, and Perth Leisure Pool all running at GBP 1.6m annual loss.
- Original plan was to close all three and build a new facility WITHOUT an ice rink, leisure pool, or indoor bowls. This caused uproar, especially from the curling community (World Curling has headquarters in Perth).
- September 2024: Council voted 20-18 to build PH2O at Thimblerow Car Park (GBP 61m). No ice rink included, but curling bodies offered chance to run Dewar's Centre for nominal rent.
- June 2025: Revised plan approved at GBP 74m, now including water play area and indoor flumes.
- The broader package includes GBP 13.2m for Crieff and Kinross swimming baths and GBP 10m to convert Bell's Sports Centre.
