# Aberdeen FC -- Comprehensive Research for Quiz Questions

## 1. FOUNDING

- **Founded:** 14 April 1903
- **How:** Merger of three Aberdeen city clubs: **Aberdeen FC (the original, founded 1881)**, **Victoria United**, and **Orion FC**
- **Catalyst for merger:** In 1902, Edinburgh-based Hibernian were considering relocating to Aberdeen and even sent a valuer to Pittodrie. This threat spurred the three local clubs to consolidate
- **Public meeting:** 20 March 1903 -- over 1,600 citizens attended a public meeting to discuss the amalgamation
- **Official formation:** 14 April 1903
- **First match:** 15 August 1903 -- a 1-1 draw vs Stenhousemuir at Pittodrie, attended by 8,000 spectators
- **First captain:** Willie McAulay, who scored Aberdeen's first Pittodrie goal
- **First trophy:** Aberdeenshire Cup, 5 March 1904 (defeated Bon Accord 3-2)
- **Original Aberdeen FC (1881):** Founded at a meeting at the Albert Hotel, Correction Wynd, on 8 October 1881 -- attended by 12 enthusiasts (8 schoolteachers, 2 bookbinders, 1 medical student, 1 tailor). The preponderance of teachers is significant for the "Dons" nickname

## 2. PITTODRIE STADIUM

### Basic facts
- **Location:** Pittodrie Street, Aberdeen
- **Opened:** 2 September 1899 (originally for the pre-merger Aberdeen FC)
- **First match at Pittodrie:** A 7-1 victory against Dumbarton, with Alex Shiach scoring the inaugural goal
- **Current capacity:** 20,866 (all seated)
- **Record attendance:** 45,061 -- Scottish Cup quarter-final vs Hearts, 13 March 1954 (Aberdeen won 3-0)

### Meaning of the name
- "Pittodrie" translates from Gaelic/Pictish as **"hill of dung"** or **"place of manure"**
- The site was literally a former dung hill for police horses, which was cleared and readied for football
- Adjacent to Trinity Cemetery

### The four stands
1. **Main Stand (North):** Built 1925-1928 by architect Archibald Leitch (the famous stadium architect). Features a distinctive granite facade. Holds over 4,000 spectators. Contains club offices, dressing rooms, media facilities. Some views obstructed by pillars
2. **Richard Donald Stand (East):** Completed 1993 at a cost of GBP 4.5 million. Two-tier cantilever construction. Accommodates over 6,000 seats with 12 executive boxes. Named after former chairman Dick Donald. Officially opened 18 August 1993
3. **Merkland Road Stand (West):** Also known as "the Paddock" or "King Street End". Family stand with reduced prices. Approximately 3,000 seats, single tier. Roof added 1934, rebuilt and officially reopened 9 October 1985
4. **South Stand:** Primarily for visiting supporters. Approximately 4,500 capacity, cantilevered single-tier design. Includes "Red Shed" safe standing section introduced for 2024/25 season

### Dugouts -- INVENTED at Pittodrie
- **Inventor:** Donald Colman (born Donald Cameron Cunningham, 14 August 1878 -- 4 October 1942)
- **Year installed:** Summer of 1931 (some sources say 1934; the AFC Heritage Trust dates it to 1931 when Colman became trainer-coach under manager Paddy Travers)
- **Inspiration:** While coaching at SK Brann in Bergen, Norway during summers, Colman observed Scandinavian managers huddling for shelter near the pitch in bad weather. He wanted to sit below pitch level to study players' footwork
- **Design:** A sunken, sheltered bench below pitch level, allowing coaches to observe players' feet and make notes without rain spoiling them
- **Spread:** Everton visited Aberdeen shortly after the dugouts were introduced and took the idea back to England. The concept then spread worldwide
- **Pittodrie was the first football stadium in the world to have dugouts**

### First all-seater stadium in the UK
- **1978:** Pittodrie became the first all-seater stadium in Britain
- **1981:** Became the UK's first all-covered, all-seater stadium
- Following a devastating fire on 6 February 1971 that destroyed approximately one-third of the Main Stand, the stadium underwent major conversion

### New stadium plans
- **Kingsford proposal (2016):** Initial plans for a GBP 40 million, 20,000-capacity stadium at Kingsford on the western edge of the city. Gained planning approval January 2018 but stalled in 2021 due to COVID-19
- **Beachfront alternative:** Plans for a multi-sports stadium near the Beach Leisure Centre, close to Pittodrie. Aberdeen City Council involved but no public funding allocated
- **Current status (2025):** Kingsford remains "plan B"; beachfront is preferred option. No firm timeline. A commissioned report projects GBP 1.6 billion economic uplift over 50 years
- Training facility at **Cormack Park** opened 31 October 2019

## 3. NICKNAMES

- **The Dons** -- Most common. Origin disputed:
  - *Teacher theory:* 8 of the 12 founders of the original 1881 club were schoolteachers; "don" means teacher/academic
  - *River Don theory:* Named after the River Don which flows through Aberdeen
  - *Abbreviation theory:* "C'mon the Aberdonians" shortened to "C'mon the Donians" then to "C'mon the Dons"
- **The Reds** -- After the red home kit adopted in 1939
- **The Dandies** / **The Dandy Dons** -- Less common, affectionate term
- **The Wasps** -- HISTORICAL nickname from the 1904-1939 era when the club wore black and gold striped shirts

## 4. THE FERGIE YEARS (1978-1986)

### Arrival
- **Appointed:** June 1978 (some sources say 1 June 1978)
- **Came from:** St Mirren (where he had been sacked despite winning promotion)
- **Age when appointed:** 36
- **First season (1978-79):** Underwhelming, finished fourth

### Trophy haul -- 10 trophies in 8 years
- **Scottish Premier Division (3):** 1979-80, 1983-84, 1984-85
- **Scottish Cup (4):** 1981-82, 1982-83, 1983-84, 1985-86
- **Scottish League Cup (1):** 1985-86
- **European Cup Winners' Cup (1):** 1982-83
- **European Super Cup (1):** 1983

### 1979-80 title significance
- First club outside the Old Firm to win the Scottish league since Kilmarnock in 1965
- Broke 15 years of Old Firm dominance

### 1983 European Cup Winners' Cup -- THE GLORY OF GOTHENBURG

**Route to the final:**
1. Preliminary Round: vs FC Sion (Switzerland) -- Won 11-1 on aggregate (7-0 home, 4-1 away)
2. First Round: vs Dinamo Tirana (Albania) -- Won 1-0 on aggregate (1-0 home, 0-0 away)
3. Second Round: vs Lech Poznan (Poland) -- Won 3-0 on aggregate (2-0 home, 1-0 away)
4. Quarter-Final: vs Bayern Munich (West Germany) -- Won 3-2 on aggregate (0-0 away, 3-2 home)
5. Semi-Final: vs Waterschei (Belgium) -- Won 5-2 on aggregate
6. **FINAL: vs Real Madrid -- WON 2-1 after extra time**

**The Final -- match details:**
- **Date:** 11 May 1983
- **Venue:** Nya Ullevi Stadium, Gothenburg, Sweden
- **Attendance:** 17,804 (some sources say 17,800)
- **Referee:** Gianfranco Menegali (Italy)
- **Weather:** Torrential rain all day. So heavy that officials covered the pitch with tarpaulin before kickoff. Referee inspected the pitch and considered calling the match off

**Aberdeen lineup (4-4-2):**
GK: Jim Leighton; DEF: Stuart Kennedy (injured, actually Doug Rougvie started), Willie Miller (c), Alex McLeish, Doug Rougvie; MID: Gordon Strachan, Neil Simpson, Neale Cooper, Peter Weir; FWD: Mark McGhee, Eric Black

*Note: Stuart Kennedy was named as substitute despite being injured, as a mark of respect from Ferguson*

**Real Madrid lineup (4-3-3):**
GK: Agustin; DEF: Juan Jose, Metgod, Bonet, Camacho; MID: Angel, Gallego, Stielike; FWD: Juanito, Santillana (c), Isidro

**Goals:**
- **7th minute:** Eric Black (Aberdeen) -- close range after Alex McLeish headed down Gordon Strachan's corner
- **15th minute:** Juanito (Real Madrid) -- penalty after Jim Leighton fouled Santillana
- **112th minute:** John Hewitt (Aberdeen) -- header from close range after Mark McGhee's cross, set up by Peter Weir winning midfield possession

**Key substitution:** Eric Black replaced by **John Hewitt** in the 87th minute. The super-sub scored the winner 25 minutes later

**Real Madrid subs:** Isidoro San Jose on for Camacho (90th min, start of extra time); Salguero on for Isidro (103rd min)

**Significance:**
- Aberdeen became only the third Scottish club to win a European trophy (after Celtic in 1967 and Rangers in 1972)
- This remains the last time Real Madrid lost a major European final
- Gordon Strachan later said: "That game is still the last time that Real Madrid lost a major European final. We were led by Sir Alex Ferguson, arguably the best manager of all time, who just scared you into winning!"

### 1983 European Super Cup -- vs Hamburg SV

- **First leg:** 22 November 1983, Volksparkstadion, Hamburg -- 0-0 draw
- **Second leg:** 20 December 1983, Pittodrie -- Aberdeen won 2-0
  - Neil Simpson scored in the 47th minute (2 minutes after halftime)
  - Mark McGhee scored in the 65th minute
  - Attendance: 22,500
- **Hamburg's squad:** Included captain **Felix Magath** (who had scored the only goal in Hamburg's European Cup final win over Juventus). Neil Simpson was tasked with man-marking Magath and nullified him
- **Aberdeen became the first Scottish club to win the European Super Cup**
- **Aberdeen lineup:** Leighton, McKimmie, McMaster, Simpson, McLeish, Miller, Strachan, Hewitt, McGhee, Bell, Weir

## 5. PRE-FERGIE HISTORY

### Early years
- **1903-1905:** Played in the Northern League
- **1905:** Promoted to Scottish First Division after finishing 7th in Second Division
- **1906:** First top-flight victory over Celtic at Pittodrie (1-0, 3 March 1906)
- **1908:** Defeated Arsenal 4-1
- **1910:** First victory over Rangers (4-2 at Ibrox, 17 September 1910)
- **1923:** Record win of 13-0 vs Peterhead (Scottish Cup, 10 February 1923)

### Dave Halliday era (1938-1955) -- The first golden age
- Manager Dave Halliday led Aberdeen to their first major trophies
- **1947 Scottish Cup:** Beat Hibernian 2-1 in the final (19 April 1947, Hampden Park). Goals from George Hamilton and Stan Williams. Over 80,000 attendance. Aberdeen's first major trophy
- **1954-55:** Won their first Scottish League title
- **1955-56 League Cup:** Won under Halliday's successor, beating St Mirren

### 1960s-70s managers
- **Eddie Turnbull (1965-1971):** Signed Joe Harper for GBP 40,000 in 1969. Aberdeen won the 1970 Scottish Cup
- **Jimmy Bonthrone (1971-1975):** Signed Zoltan Varga from Hertha Berlin in 1972
- **Ally MacLeod (1975-1977):** Won the 1976-77 League Cup. Left to manage Scotland
- **Billy McNeill (1977-1978):** Brief spell before moving to Celtic. Preceded Ferguson

## 8. RIVALRIES

### vs Rangers (and Celtic -- Old Firm)
- Aberdeen's rivalry with Rangers is a comparatively modern phenomenon, developed from the 1970s
- Intensified massively during the Ferguson years when Aberdeen broke Old Firm dominance
- Ferguson's record vs Rangers in league: Won 17, Drew 12, Lost 4. Went undefeated against Rangers in all three title-winning seasons
- Key flashpoint: 1980 League Cup match -- Willie Johnston stamped on John McMaster's neck at Ibrox; McMaster had to be given the kiss of life on the pitch
- 1988 flashpoint: Neil Simpson's horror tackle on Ian Durrant snapped his cruciate ligament
- Aberdeen are considered Rangers' most bitter rivals outside the Old Firm

### vs Dundee United -- "The New Firm"
- Term coined in the 1980s when both clubs challenged the Old Firm simultaneously
- From 1983-1985, three consecutive league titles went to either Aberdeen or Dundee United
- North-east Scotland derby
- Aberdeen's 1980s haul: 3 league titles, 4 Scottish Cups, 2 League Cups, ECWC, Super Cup
- Dundee United's 1980s haul: 1 league title (1983), 2 League Cups, reached 1987 UEFA Cup Final, reached 1984 European Cup semi-final
- The only period in Scottish football history where two clubs outside Glasgow simultaneously dominated

### The Highland Derby / North Derby (vs Inverness Caledonian Thistle)
- Despite being over 100 miles apart
- First competitive meeting: Scottish League Cup, 8 August 1998
- Rivalry developed after Inverness won promotion to SPL in 2004
- Aberdeen had a long unbeaten record against Inverness, often winning with very late goals
- Record broken: Opening day of 2008-09 season when Inverness won 2-0 at Pittodrie
- Most high-profile meeting: 2014 Scottish League Cup Final -- Aberdeen won on penalties (0-0 after extra time)

## 10. COLOURS AND KIT

### Home kit history
- **1886-1888:** First wore black and gold stripes
- **1904-1939:** Black and gold stripes (the "Wasps" era). Blue shorts until 1911, then white
- **29 April 1939:** Last match in black and gold (2-0 over Rangers)
- **12 August 1939:** First match in red and white (3-1 over Celtic)
- **1966-present:** All-red home kit adopted
- The change to red and white in 1939 reflected the silver and red colours of the official City of Aberdeen coat of arms

### Away kit
- Traditionally white with black shorts
- Yellow and black variations used (especially for European games)
- All-white for 2007-08 season
- In the 1970s, wore all-blue away
- **2024-25:** "Wasp Kit" -- black and gold, honouring the heritage

## 11. BADGE/CREST

- **No official crest until 1972**
- **November 1972:** First official crest unveiled, designed by Aberdonian graphic designer **Donald Addison**
- **Design:** Capital letter "A" depicted as the side view of a football goal, with a ball forming the crossbar of the "A". The ball was crosshatched to look like it was inside the net (signifying a goal). Letters "FC" below
- **Mid-1980s:** Words "Aberdeen Football Club" added in a circle around the badge, founding year 1903 added
- **1997-98:** Current version of the crest introduced
- **2005-06:** Two red stars added above the badge to commemorate the two European trophies won in 1983 (Cup Winners' Cup and Super Cup)
- Aberdeen are the only Scottish club to display two European trophy stars on their crest

## 12. SONGS AND CHANTS

- **"The Northern Lights of Old Aberdeen"** -- The club's signature anthem. Based on a traditional Scottish song about Aberdeen. Features nostalgic lyrics about the city, home, and memories of Pittodrie. Made famous by singer Kenneth McKellar
- **"Stand Free"** -- Popular matchday chant
- **"Oh When the Reds Go Steaming In"** -- Terrace classic
- **"The European Song"** -- Celebrating the 1983 triumph

## 13. EUROPEAN RECORD -- COMPLETE OVERVIEW

- **Total European campaigns:** 36 seasons since 1967
- **First European match:** 1967-68 Cup Winners' Cup vs KR Reykjavik (won 10-0 -- Aberdeen's record European win)
- **European trophies:** 2 (ECWC 1983, Super Cup 1983) -- a Scottish record
- **Only Scottish team to win two European trophies**

### Competition breakdown:
- **European Cup:** Entered 3 times. Best: quarter-final in 1985-86
- **Cup Winners' Cup:** Entered 8 times. Won 1982-83. Semi-final the following season (1983-84)
- **UEFA Cup / Europa League / Fairs Cup:** 17 appearances. Best: third round (twice)

### European firsts and records:
- **1970-71:** Aberdeen became the FIRST club in history to be eliminated from European competition via a penalty shootout -- losing to Hungarian side Honved (5-4 on penalties after 4-4 aggregate). The referee didn't even know about the new penalty shootout rule and tried to arrange a third match, until a European observer informed him
- Jim Forrest missed the crucial penalty (hit the bar), and the Honved goalkeeper scored the winning kick

## 14. BIZARRE AND UNUSUAL FACTS

1. **"Place of manure"** -- Their home ground's name literally means "hill of dung" in Gaelic

4. **100% record vs Real Madrid** -- Aberdeen have played Real Madrid once and won. They are one of very few clubs Real Madrid have never beaten

7. **Nearly moved to Aberdeen... from Edinburgh** -- The whole club only exists because Hibernian threatened to relocate to Aberdeen in 1902, prompting the merger
8. **The Wasps** -- For 35 years (1904-1939) they played in black and gold stripes and were called The Wasps, not The Reds
9. **Zoltan Varga** -- An Olympic gold medallist and banned German football player ended up at Pittodrie for one magical season
10. **George Best needed a stiff drink** after watching Varga's masterclass against Manchester United at Pittodrie in 1972
11. **Stuart Kennedy named as substitute in Gothenburg despite being injured** -- Ferguson's mark of respect so Kennedy could be "part of" the final
12. **Aberdeen have won more European trophies (2) than all but two Scottish clubs combined** (Celtic: 1, Rangers: 1)
13. **The 1947 Scottish Cup final** -- Their first ever major trophy, the starting lineup was watched by over 80,000
14. **Ferguson's quote about leaving:** "The Old Trafford job was something I could not turn down" -- and he went on to become the most successful manager in English football history

## 16. THE GOTHENBURG GAME IN DETAIL

### Pre-match
- The rain was so torrential all day that the match was in doubt
- Officials covered the Nya Ullevi pitch with tarpaulin to try to protect it
- Referee Gianfranco Menegali (Italy) inspected the pitch and considered calling it off
- An estimated 12,000 Aberdeen fans made the trip to Gothenburg -- remarkable for a city of 200,000
- Real Madrid were heavy favourites, featuring internationals like Stielike (Germany), Camacho, Juanito, and Santillana

### The match
- **7th min:** ABERDEEN 1-0. Strachan corner, McLeish headed down, Eric Black scored from close range
- **15th min:** 1-1. Leighton fouled Santillana in the box, Juanito converted the penalty
- Rest of first half: Real Madrid dominant with approximately 55% possession
- Second half: Aberdeen grew into the game, Jim Leighton made pivotal saves
- **87th min:** Key substitution -- Ferguson replaced the tiring Eric Black with John Hewitt
- Full-time: 1-1

### Extra time
- Real Madrid visibly fatigued; Aberdeen's younger, fitter players took control
- **Real Madrid sub (90th min):** San Jose on for Camacho
- **Real Madrid sub (103rd min):** Salguero on for Isidro
- **112th min:** THE WINNING GOAL. Peter Weir won possession in midfield. Ball played to Mark McGhee who crossed from the right. **John Hewitt** met it with a diving header from close range. 2-1 Aberdeen
- Aberdeen held on for the remaining 8 minutes

### Post-match
- Scenes of wild celebration. Ferguson and Mark McGhee held aloft the trophy
- The team returned home by boat to a heroes' welcome
- The 12 players who featured became known as the "Gothenburg Greats"
- Aberdeen became only the third Scottish club to win a European trophy
